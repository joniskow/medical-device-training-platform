## Source photos: two different doors

The original normalized photographs are in `output/nxstage-system-one-photos-normalized/`. The upper cycler compartment and the lower black cabinet are separate assemblies; their doors should not be conflated.

- `20261020_130849.jpg` shows the lower cabinet closed, including the rectangular viewing opening.
- `20261020_130843.jpg` and `20261020_130845.jpg` show the lower cabinet door open toward the viewer's left. The door pivots at the cabinet's left vertical edge and exposes the lower control unit, connector strip, and gray container.
- `20261020_131015.jpg` and `20261020_131018.jpg` show the lower interior from the front, including the removable container.
- `20261020_131101.jpg` and `20261020_131048.jpg` show the side of the upper cycler and its support/handle assembly, but do not establish an open cycler compartment or its movement.
- `20261020_131233.jpg` is a close front view of the cycler controls. It can be used to review control placement and label shapes.

The lower cabinet is now a shell with a separately hinged door, viewing opening, and approximate container and controls behind it. Its exact hinge position, opening angle, thickness, interior details, and interaction force remain estimates until measured. The browser check verifies outward opening, closing, and the existing control targets; physical fidelity still requires source comparison and review.

The Blender generator and `pnpm nxstage-model:manifest` now target the interactive asset by default. The manifest builder updates the interactive scene binding and leaves the separately baked cinematic source alone. For an isolated build, set `NXSTAGE_OUTPUT_ROOT` and then pass its `fixtures/nxstage-system-one-interactive` directory as `NXSTAGE_MODEL_FOLDER` to the manifest and browser checks. `NXSTAGE_SKIP_RENDER=1` skips the still-image render during iteration.

The handle uses an estimated horizontal hinge. Its movement, interaction order, keyboard controls, upward mouse/touch drag, and canceled-drag behavior were checked in Chrome; the handle dimensions and real chamber details still need physical review. The same handle/door dependency is checked when authoritative session actions are submitted and when persisted model state is restored, so the text and 3D paths cannot accept an open door with its handle lowered.

The creator preview also exposes a named control list for people who cannot or prefer not to target the 3D geometry. Those buttons use the same local interaction paths and report the same handle/door dependency; they are preview actions and do not simulate treatment settings.

## Evidence for future authoring

- NxStage's [equipment redeployment training material](https://ww3.nxstage.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/All_Transitional_Dialysis_Care_Resources.pdf) includes an interior cycler photograph, according to the indexed description. The image could not be retrieved for visual inspection in this session, so it is a lead for further reference gathering, not evidence for specific geometry.
- The manufacturer’s [System One setup video, door chapter](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YTq52j9ViSk&t=88s) shows the same overall cycler form as the source photos. Around 1:27 the front control housing is closed; around 1:32 the front housing is pulled toward the user, exposing a black inner section, and the black transverse door handle is raised across the front. This gives direct visual evidence that the control fascia travels with the door and that the handle and door have distinct motions. The video does not provide measured travel, a clear view of the guide rails, or the exact interior geometry, so those remain estimates.
- The [Manitoba Renal Program’s System One setup procedure](https://www.kidneyhealth.ca/wp/wp-content/uploads/50.20.08.pdf) also describes raising the handle until it clicks and pulling the door open while keeping the handle up. This supports the order of interaction for System One specifically.
- The [NxStage nurse guide](https://ww4.nxstage.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/NurseGuide.pdf), “Cycler and Associated Components,” identifies a cycler door handle separately from the carry handle and control panel. It describes pressure sensors, air detectors, pumps, and cartridge chamber locations behind the open cycler door.
- That guide identifies the green, yellow and red arrow rows as dialysate, ultrafiltration and blood-flow adjustments and says the symbols next to the upward arrows are pictures, not buttons. The Add Fluid and Volume Toggle keys are separate controls on the left of the photographed panel. The isolated candidate follows that distinction, but the active asset and shared generator still use the older mapping. Device operation and values are not simulated.
- The manufacturer's [VersiHD reference guide](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/content/dam/fresenius-medical-care/global/en/02_healthcare-professionals/pdf/services_resources/educational-resources/TM1184_NxStage_VersiHD_Reference_Guide__MT-EN__1_.pdf) describes raising the front handle until it clicks and then pulling the door toward the operator. This is a useful reference for a related model, but does not establish the handle geometry, travel, or compartment details of the photographed System One variant.
- The manufacturer's [System One education page](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/en-us/products/treating-with-home-hemodialysis/training-resources/education-videos/) lists setup and use videos that may provide motion reference.
- The [System One quick reference guide](https://freseniusmedicalcare.com/content/dam/fresenius-medical-care/global/en/02_healthcare-professionals/pdf/services_resources/educational-resources/NxStage_System_One_Quick_Reference_Guide__TM1535__MT-EN.pdf) identifies the cartridge and cycler in the setup sequence; it is not sufficient by itself to establish the door's hinge geometry or dimensions.
- [NxStage patent US6979309B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6979309B2/en) describes a door moving toward and away from a chassis on two rails, with a cartridge placed in the slot between them. This establishes a plausible *sliding* mechanism for an NxStage design, not proof that the photographed specific System One variant uses the same geometry or travel. Its figures 2 and 4–6 warrant comparison with a variant-matched open-door reference before authoring.

To author a credible upper cycler door, gather views of the same device variant closed, unlatched, partially open, and fully open; establish the handle and door movement axes, their travel limits and order, and the geometry exposed behind the door. In particular, distinguish a rail-guided translation from the lower cabinet's hinged rotation. Record measurements or mark them as estimates. Author separate movable nodes with stable IDs, then verify motion and occlusion from several camera angles. Do not infer a dial merely from a circular shape: verify its function and travel against the device variant and source evidence.
